Scheme overview

The Hardy Orchid Society (HOS) promotes the conservation, cultivation, scientific study and sustainable enjoyment of the wild orchids of Britain, Europe and other temperate regions. We are committed to safeguarding wild orchid populations and protecting the habitats on which they depend for the benefit of current and future generations.

The HOS Conservation Grant Scheme supports projects that protect, restore and enhance habitats for wild orchids. Through small grants, HOS encourages organisations, community groups and individuals to incorporate orchid conservation into their wider environmental initiatives.

Successful applicants will be asked to acknowledge receipt of funding and, on completion of their project, provide a brief report confirming how the grant was used. Photographs of the completed work are also welcomed where possible.

Eligibility

  1. Funding is available to a wide range of applicants, including charities, community groups, schools, voluntary organisations, special interest groups and individuals.
  2. Projects must be located entirely within the United Kingdom. Domestic garden projects are not eligible.
  3. Projects must be underway or scheduled to begin within 12 months of the grant award.
  4. HOS may provide additional funding to projects that have already secured support from other funding bodies where such funding would enhance conservation outcomes.
  5. Applicants may submit only one application per year. Multiple applications from the same applicant will not be considered.
  6. Applications must clearly state how the funding will be used and include costs for specific equipment, including make and model where applicable, together with quotations for any contracted work.

Successful applicants must provide annual progress updates until the project is completed and a final report has been submitted. HOS may also provide a plaque for display at the project site. Unless there are exceptional reasons not to do so, HOS will publicise successful awards.

Applicants may also be invited to contribute a short article to the HOS Journal or present their project at an HOS meeting, helping to share the outcomes and benefits of their work with the wider orchid community.

If you are unsure whether your project is eligible, please contact hosgrants@hardyorchidsociety.org with a brief outline of your proposal and a contact telephone number for an informal discussion.

Application guidelines

The application form requests details about the applicant, organisation (if applicable), contact information, project location and the amount of funding sought. This must be accompanied by a project proposal.

Completed applications should be submitted by email attachment to hosgrants@hardyorchidsociety.org. All applications will be acknowledged. Decisions made by HOS are final.

Applications will be assessed against the following criteria:

  1. The project must support the conservation, promotion or sustainable enjoyment of native wild orchids and the protection or sensitive management of their habitats.
  2. The application should clearly describe the project's objectives and expected conservation outcomes.
  3. Each application will be considered on its individual merits.

Application requirements

Applications must be submitted electronically and include:

  • A completed application form.
  • A separate project description of one to two pages in PDF or Word format.
  • A font size no smaller than 12-point Calibri or Times New Roman.

The project description should address the following areas:

  1. Current condition of the site: describe habitat type, soil conditions, land-use history and relevant previous agricultural activity.
  2. Flora and existing orchid populations: describe species present and whether orchid populations are stable, increasing or declining.
  3. Proposed project: explain how grant funding will be used, describe proposed measures, and include a cost breakdown.
  4. Long-term management: outline ongoing plans to maintain and enhance conservation outcomes.
  5. Public access and community engagement: describe access and how local stakeholders will be involved.
  6. Vision for the future: set out expected benefits for orchids and their habitats over the short and long term.

Supporting information

Please include:

  • At least one photograph of the site (maximum of five photographs, each no larger than 2.5 MB).
  • An illustrative map of the project area, where available.

All information should be submitted as email attachments. Please do not send links to external file-sharing services.
