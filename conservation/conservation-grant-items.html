Conservation

What Can HOS Conservation Grants Fund?

These examples are guidance only and are not exhaustive. They indicate the types of expenditure likely to be supported, and examples of costs that are unlikely to be funded.

If you are unsure whether your proposed project or expenditure is eligible, please contact hosgrants@hardyorchidsociety.org for advice before submitting a full application.

Examples of eligible expenditure

HOS Conservation Grants are intended to support practical conservation activities that benefit wild orchids and their habitats. Examples of expenditure that may be considered include:

Habitat management tools and equipment

  • Hand tools used for vegetation management, including scythes, loppers, tree poppers, crowbars, shears, secateurs and hand saws.
  • Battery-powered tools such as strimmers, together with necessary batteries and chargers.
  • Petrol-powered tools where justified by the scale or nature of the project.
  • Trugs, sacks and containers used for the removal and transportation of cut vegetation.

Volunteer safety and training

  • Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), including hard hats, safety boots, gloves, goggles and protective face masks.
  • Training for volunteers in the safe use of equipment directly associated with the proposed conservation project.

Habitat protection

  • Protective fencing around orchid-rich areas to prevent damage from grazing animals.
  • Individual plant guards where orchid populations are scattered across a site.
  • Fence posts, netting, wire and associated materials required for habitat protection.
  • Tools required for fencing work, such as post-hole spades, post drivers, hammers and wire cutters.

Site management

  • Secure tool storage units used to house project equipment.
  • The hire of specialist contractors or machinery for scrub clearance on orchid sites, where alternative approaches have been considered and are not practical.

Education and interpretation

  • Information boards, signage and educational materials that help promote awareness and appreciation of wild orchids and their conservation.

Legal and regulatory requirements

Where funding is provided for equipment or activities requiring specific licences, permits, qualifications or permissions, it is the responsibility of the grant recipient to ensure all legal requirements are met. This may include, but is not limited to:

  • Chainsaw certification and operator training.
  • Compliance with wildlife protection legislation.
  • Restrictions relating to bird nesting seasons.
  • Tree Preservation Orders (TPOs).
  • Landowner permissions and statutory consents.

Receipt of a grant does not remove the recipient's responsibility to comply with relevant legislation and best practice.

Examples of expenditure unlikely to be funded

The following items would not normally be considered eligible for HOS Conservation Grant funding:

  • Consultancy fees.
  • Site access improvements, including roads and pathways.
  • Car park construction or enhancement.
  • Site lighting.
  • Power supplies to existing buildings.
  • General landscaping projects.
  • Site drainage schemes.
  • Picnic tables, benches and other visitor seating.
  • Public waste and litter bins.
  • General rubbish clearance and waste removal.
  • Buildings, offices, lecture facilities or meeting rooms.
  • Office furniture, storage systems and filing cabinets.
  • Toilet facilities.
  • Soil testing.
  • Habitat surveys undertaken solely to assess proposed orchid reintroductions.
  • Lobbying, campaigning or marketing activities.
  • IT equipment, software, mobile phones or software licences.
  • Staff salaries associated with report writing or administrative duties.

Conditions of grant awards

  • HOS may publicise successful grant awards unless there are compelling reasons not to do so.
  • Grant recipients must provide evidence that the funded work or purchases have been completed. This may include receipts, photographs, videos or other agreed forms of verification.
  • Recipients will be encouraged to share the outcomes of their project through a short article in the HOS Journal or a presentation at an HOS meeting.
  • Grant payments will normally be made by electronic bank transfer following agreement of the payment arrangements.
